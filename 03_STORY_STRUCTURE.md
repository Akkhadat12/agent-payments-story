# When an AI Spends for Us

## Approved direction

Owner approved thesis 1 on 30 September 2026, with production-input examples from option 2 in the middle. The audience-facing question is: When an AI spends for us, how much can we trust it?

**Thesis:** Agents are expanding from helping people decide to purchasing consumer goods and production inputs, but scaling this activity depends as much on bounded delegation, spending control and verification of outcomes as on convenient payments.

Audience: general Thai viewers interested in technology and business. Research cutoff: 30 September 2026. Spoken language: Thai. Website text: minimal English. This file defines content and relationships, not final artwork, palette or precise camera choreography.

## Arc and human relevance

Begin with an everyday decision: a hypothetical owner delegates a shoe purchase with a THB 3,000 total budget. Ask what “under budget” leaves unsaid. Separate the permission to decide from the mechanism that moves money. Ground the discussion in current commerce paths that still keep merchants and human approval involved. Then move from buying a finished good to buying small inputs needed to produce work. Examine total task cost and the evidence gap between payment counts and autonomous demand. Close on the checks that make delegation worth trusting.

The concrete stakes are the owner's money, the merchant's ability to fulfill the right order, and a business's cost of completing useful work. Do not invent a named shopper, actual financial loss, or a forecast of job displacement. The shoe budget is an illustrative Thai-language bridge, not a Thai product-availability claim.

## S05 A cheap call is not a cheap task

- Time: 75 seconds
- Main idea: Economic usefulness depends on the whole successful task, including retries and checking
- Spatial relationship: small paid inputs accumulate into a task-cost stack alongside inference, retry and review burdens. A completed output must pass a quality check before the cost is meaningful
- Evidence: C14/C17; original conditional analysis; the only source price used is [Coinbase SQL](https://www.coinbase.com/developer-platform/discover/launches/sql-api-x402)
- Talking points: ten $0.10 queries make $1 in data fees, not a $1 finished report; an existing subscription may still be better; savings require measuring outcomes
- Copy budget: 12 words excluding arithmetic labels: `Illustrative`, `10 × $0.10 = $1`, `Data only`, `Inference`, `Retries`, `Review`, `Useful result`
- Advance: activate the aggregated payment count/cost record, zooming out into market evidence S06
- Boundary: do not assign invented amounts to inference/retries/review or show fake measured proportions; no cost-benefit outcome is claimed

## S07 Trust is a chain of checks

- Time: 75 seconds
- Main idea: Even authorized payments must be connected to the intended recipient, delivered output and a recovery process
- Spatial relationship: follow one conceptual purchase across permission, payment, delivery and resolution. A failed delivery visibly stops completion while the payment record remains; review/cancel routes stay linked to the responsible owner
- Evidence: C04/C12/C13; [security research](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19545v1), [IMF Note](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/imf-notes/2026/english/insea2026004.pdf)
- Talking points: authorized does not mean fulfilled; budgets need purpose and recipient checks; published vulnerabilities were studied on older versions and mitigations followed; legal responsibility remains case-specific
- Copy budget: 10 words: `Authorized`, `Paid`, `Delivered?`, `Review`, `Stop`, `Resolve`
- Advance: activate the verified outcome/owner checkpoint, widening the camera back to both purchase types in S08
- Boundary: conceptual failure, not an allegation of a current breach; no universal automatic refund guarantee

## S08 Conclusion — Delegate within bounds

- Time: 50 seconds
- Main idea: The practical question is which decisions to delegate, within what boundaries, with what evidence of success
- Spatial relationship: both consumer purchase and production-input task remain recognizable; a shared authorization and outcome record links them back to the human/organization's goal
- Claim: approved conditional thesis, C01/C14/C17
- Talking points: neither “AI buys everything” nor “nothing is real”; ask what is allowed, total cost, outcome, and how to stop or resolve trouble
